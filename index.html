Achieving Expressive Emotional Text-to-Speech with Minimal Architecture Complexity

Kor Jia Hong, Abdul Hadi Mohamada, Husna Sarirah Husina, Adinath M

Abstract

This study developed a lightweight emotional TTS framework for synthesizing expressive speech with lower storage overhead. Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) was used to create emotion-specific adapters, and only the LoRA adapter weights were updated while the base VITS components were kept frozen. The proposed model occupied 0.29 MB of storage per emotion adapter and trained approximately 0.54% of the baseline trainable parameters per adapter while maintaining a comparable real-time factor (RTF). However, the model showed quality trade-offs for high-arousal emotions, particularly Happy and Angry, as reflected in higher WER and CER scores. The model also achieved competitive E-SIM scores, while the low S-SIM values indicate a speaker-preservation limitation that may also be affected by the speaker-embedding evaluator and pairing protocol. Overall, the study shows that LoRA can reduce storage and training complexity for emotional TTS, but further work is required to improve pronunciation stability and speaker preservation.
